## 1. Loan Product Marketing & Borrower Education

**1. Loan Program Explainer**
> Explain the `[FHA / VA / CONVENTIONAL / USDA / JUMBO]` loan program in plain
> English for a `[FIRST-TIME BUYER / MOVE-UP BUYER / VETERAN]`. Cover who it's
> best for, typical down payment, and 3 pros and 2 cons. Avoid quoting specific
> rates; keep it educational and compliant.
*Use this when a borrower asks "which loan is right for me?"*

**2. First-Time Buyer Financing Guide**
> Write a friendly mini-guide for first-time buyers in `[STATE]` covering the
> mortgage steps: pre-approval, house hunting, application, underwriting, closing.
> Reassuring, jargon-free, with a CTA to get pre-approved.
*Use this as a lead magnet or onboarding handout.*

**3. Market & Rate Commentary (compliant)**
> Write a short, compliant market-update post about current mortgage conditions
> for `[AREA]`. Discuss trends and what they mean for buyers in general terms
> WITHOUT quoting specific rates or APRs. Encourage readers to request a
> personalized quote.
*Use this for your weekly/monthly rate-environment content.*

**4. Affordability Explainer**
> Explain how much home a buyer earning `[INCOME]` with `[MONTHLY DEBTS]` might
> afford, walking through DTI, down payment, and monthly-payment components in
> plain English. Use it as education, not a commitment, and recommend a full
> pre-approval.
*Use this when buyers ask "how much can I afford?"*

**5. Refinance Benefit Explainer**
> Write a clear explanation of when refinancing may make sense (rate/term, cash-
> out, removing PMI) for a homeowner with `[SCENARIO]`. Balanced, no pressure,
> compliant, with a CTA to run their numbers.
*Use this when nurturing past clients about refi opportunities.*

**6. Down Payment Options Explainer**
> Summarize down payment options and assistance programs that may be available in
> `[STATE]` (low-down-payment loans, DPA, gift funds). Keep it general and
> encourage verifying eligibility with me.
*Use this when budget-conscious buyers think they can't afford to buy.*

**7. Credit Tips for Borrowers**
> Write 7 actionable, accurate tips a borrower can use to strengthen their credit
> profile before applying for a mortgage. Encouraging tone; note that I can review
> their specific situation.
*Use this when a lead isn't quite mortgage-ready yet.*

**8. Pre-Approval vs. Pre-Qualification**
> Explain the difference between pre-qualification and pre-approval in simple
> terms, why pre-approval matters in a competitive offer, and how to start. Short
> and shareable.
*Use this to convert casual leads into pre-approval applications.*

**9. Self-Employed Borrower Guide**
> Write a reassuring guide for self-employed / 1099 borrowers on how mortgage
> qualifying works for them, what documents help, and common solutions. Avoid
> guarantees; invite a personalized review.
*Use this to attract an underserved, high-value borrower segment.*

**10. VA Loan Benefits for Veterans**
> Write a respectful, informative post on the benefits of VA loans for eligible
> veterans and service members, covering key advantages in general terms with a
> CTA to check eligibility.
*Use this when marketing to military and veteran communities.*

**34. Conditional Approval Explainer**

**35. Appraisal Results Communication**

**37. Clear-to-Close Announcement**

**39. Post-Closing Thank You + Review/Referral**
> Write a warm post-closing thank-you to `[NAME]`, naturally request a review with

**40. Denial / Alternative-Path Email**
> Write an empathetic, compliant email to `[NAME]` whose application can't be

*Use this when a loan can't move forward yet.*

## 5. Objections, Compliance & Business Growth

**41. Objection: "Another Lender Quoted Lower"**
> Write a confident, ethical response to a borrower who got a lower quote
> elsewhere. Focus on comparing the full picture (fees, terms, service, on-time
> closing) rather than attacking competitors, and offer to review their estimate
> side by side.
*Use this when a borrower is rate-shopping.*

**42. Objection: "I'll Wait for Rates to Drop"**
> Write a balanced response to buyers waiting for lower rates. Acknowledge the
> concern, discuss trade-offs (price/competition vs. rate) generally, and offer a
> personalized game plan — without predicting rate movements.
*Use this when timing hesitation stalls a buyer.*

**43. Objection: "Why So Many Fees?"**
> Write a clear, transparent explanation of common mortgage closing costs and what
> each is for, reframing them as the cost of getting into the home. Honest and
> reassuring.
*Use this when a borrower is surprised by closing costs.*

**44. Rate-Shopping Retention**
> Write a message to retain a borrower who's tempted to switch lenders late in the
> process, emphasizing relationship, reliability, and the risk of restarting.
> Respectful, not pushy.
*Use this when a deal is at risk of poaching.*

**45. Explaining a Higher Payment**
> Write an email helping `[NAME]` understand why their estimated payment is higher
> than expected (taxes, insurance, PMI, HOA), breaking down each component clearly.
*Use this when payment shock needs a calm explanation.*

**46. Referral Request to Realtors**
> Write a natural script (text + email) asking realtor partners for referrals,
> being specific about the buyers I serve best: `[IDEAL BORROWER]`, and reminding
> them of my fast, communicative service.
*Use this when systematically generating partner referrals.*

**47. 90-Day Partnership Plan**
> Build a 90-day plan to land `[GOAL: e.g., 3 new referring agents]`. Break it into
> weekly outreach, value-add touches (lunch-and-learns, co-marketing), and a simple
> tracking method.
*Use this when you need structure to grow referrals.*

**48. Monthly Newsletter (borrowers + partners)**
> Write a monthly newsletter with two short sections: one for past clients (a
> useful homeowner/financing tip) and one for agent partners (a market note +ways
> I help their buyers). Friendly and brief.
*Use this when staying top-of-mind with your whole database.*

**49. Annual Mortgage Review Outreach**
> Write an outreach message offering past clients a free "annual mortgage review"
> to check whether their loan still fits their goals, positioning me as a lifelong
> resource.
*Use this when re-engaging your past-client book each year.*

**50. Review Response Templates**
> Write professional responses to reviews: 2 for glowing 5-star reviews (gracious,
> specific) and 2 for a critical review `[PASTE REVIEW]` (empathetic, solution-
> oriented, never defensive or sharing private loan details).
*Use this when managing your online reputation.*
